## Question

If \(xy > 0\) and \(x + y < 0\), which of the following must be true?

## Explanation

Read the two conditions one at a time.

\(xy > 0\) tells you \(x\) and \(y\) have the **same sign** — either both positive or both negative.
(It also rules out either being \(0\), since that would make the product \(0\), not positive.)

\(x + y < 0\) tells you their sum is negative. Two positive numbers can never sum to something
negative, so the "both positive" case is out. That leaves only **both negative**, which is
answer **A**.

**The traps:**

- **B** (both positive) contradicts \(x + y < 0\).
- **C** (opposite signs) and **D** (\(xy < 0\)) both describe \(xy < 0\), the opposite of what is
  given.
- **E** (one is zero) is impossible: a zero factor makes \(xy = 0\), not \(xy > 0\).

## Hints

- \(xy > 0\) means the two numbers share a sign; \(xy < 0\) means opposite signs.
- If they share a sign but add to a negative number, which sign must it be?
