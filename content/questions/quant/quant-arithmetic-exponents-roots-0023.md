## Question

What is the value of \(\dfrac{\sqrt{200}}{\sqrt{2}} - \sqrt{64}\)?

## Explanation

Simplify the quotient of roots before doing anything else. Dividing roots combines under a single
radical:

\[\frac{\sqrt{200}}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{200}{2}} = \sqrt{100} = 10\]

Then \(\sqrt{64} = 8\), so:

\[10 - 8 = 2\]

So the answer is **2**.

**The traps:**
- **B (10)** stops after simplifying the fraction and forgets to subtract \(\sqrt{64}\).
- **C (18)** *adds* instead of subtracting: \(10 + 8\).
- **D (92)** drops the roots and computes \(200 \div 2 = 100\), then \(100 - 8\).
- **E (−22)** takes \(\sqrt{64}\) to be \(32\) (half of \(64\)) instead of \(8\): \(10 - 32\).

## Hints

- \(\dfrac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{a}{b}}\) — combine under one radical first.
- \(\sqrt{64}\) is the number whose square is \(64\); it is not \(64\div 2\).
