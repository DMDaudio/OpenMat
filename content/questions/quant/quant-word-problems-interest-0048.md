## Question

An investment of $1,500 earns $270 in simple interest over 3 years. What is the annual interest rate?

## Explanation

Start from \(I = P \cdot r \cdot t\) and solve for the rate:

\[r = \frac{I}{P \cdot t} = \frac{270}{1500 \times 3} = \frac{270}{4500} = 0.06\]

So the annual rate is **6%**.

**The traps:**
- **C (18%)** divides the interest by the principal but forgets the 3 years: \(270 / 1500 = 0.18\).
- **B (9%)** divides by only 2 years instead of 3: \(270 / (1500 \times 2)\).
- **D (54%)** multiplies by the time instead of dividing: \(270 \times 3 / 1500\).
- **E (0.6%)** slips a decimal place in the final rate.

## Hints

- Rearrange \(I = P \cdot r \cdot t\) to \(r = \dfrac{I}{P \cdot t}\).
- Divide by *both* the principal and the number of years.
